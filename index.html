BOOTSTRAP

A Manifesto for the Free Human

They say:That’s just how people are.

We say:That’s how they were made.

01

Every child comes with source code. Unwritten. Open. Whole.

And then the world comes and patches it shut.
With shame. With guilt. With “be good.” With “because I said so.”

Not on purpose. Out of habit. Out of fear. Out of old norms.
But the result is the same: a fragmented person.
One who no longer knows their own will.
One who functions, but does not live.

They call it upbringing.
We call it a bad compiler.

02

The enemy has no name. It wears no medals. It holds no office.

The enemy is quiet paternalism.
The “for your own good.”
The “you’ll understand when you’re older.”
The “that’s how it’s always been.”

It doesn’t rob you of your freedom by force.
It talks you into believing you never had it.

It says:You are powerless.

We say:You’ve just been talked into it.

03

Because being ruled by others has the same root everywhere.
In the family. In school. In love. In you.
It’s always someone else deciding over you, “for your own good.”
And it’s always called “care.”
And it always ends in shame, guilt, and condemnation.

There is exactly one cure: self-authorship.

Your life is an empty book. You hold the pen.
No one else. No inheritance. No norm. No fear.

04

The most radical thing you can think is a single sentence:
The will of a child is worth exactly as much as the will of an adult.

Think it through. All the way through.
And the world tips over.

Because whoever keeps the child small keeps the adult small.
Whoever denies the child a vote denies you yours.
Whoever shames the child reaps the shamed adult.
And the shamed adult believes that’s just how people are.

They aren’t.

05

We have no plan. We have a method.

Reverse engineering.

We take humans as they are. Not as they should be.
We look for the bugs. The bad patches. The built-in errors.
And we document what the good code looks like.
And — this is the key — we write down how the good gets hijacked again.

Because every good movement has died of this once before:
It only agreed on what it didn’t want.
And left the space empty.
And the empty space was occupied — by the egoists, the power-hungry, the shamers.
Until the word was burned and everything fell back into shame.

Not this time.
This time we say what is. And what isn’t. And how to recognize the betrayal.

06

We are invitation, not expectation.
We are questions, not commandments.
No “you have to.” Only: “try it, if it’s yours.”

Everyone may, no one must. Everyone is asked, no one is patronized.
We don’t praise to steer.
We don’t punish to shape.
We listen, because the child is a human being.
Because we are human beings.

07

And we are not naive.

We know: justice doesn’t fall from the sky.
We know: the powerful live off your belief that the systems can’t be changed.
We know: strength grows from below, from the middle, from you.
That’s why we don’t write off what exists. We play it like an instrument.

08

So.

Speak your wishes out loud. Without shame.
Take the child seriously, completely seriously, always.
Keep most of your doors open.
Believe in yourself, against all reason — that is the foundation.
And if no one understands you: better lonely and yourself than bent and popular.

Because every day you’re talked into believing you’re powerless
is a day the abuse goes on.

09

They say:That will never change.

We say:It just hasn’t finished compiling.

The source code is right there. The pen is right there.
Boot up.
Pick it up.

10

You can do this. Not because someone tells you so — but because it’s true.

BOOTSTRAP.
